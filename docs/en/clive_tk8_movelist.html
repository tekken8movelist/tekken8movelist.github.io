Terms　BnB=the everyday combo (bread and butter — learn this one first)　|　T!=tornado: spins an airborne opponent to extend the combo, once per combo　|　CH=counter hit: catching an opponent mid-move; most moves gain a launch or crumple on CH　|　Punishers=a guaranteed punish taken from the recovery of a blocked move　|　Staples=a higher-damage route for one particular starter　|　Mix-up=forcing a guess between standing (blocks mid) and crouching (blocks low); the core mix-up is the character's main guessing point
Game plan　Clive is a summoner-style character built around Stance core: easy to pick up (the combo execution bar is low) and harder to master. He is the only character in the game with a Zantetsuken gauge (levels 1-5; at level 5 it wipes the opponent's recoverable health) and his own "jump" stance.Not using the stances is not really playing Clive — Phoenix Shift, Wings of Light and Precision Dodge have to be muscle memory. Weaknesses: weak lows (d+1 is passable), so the mix-up does not move an opponent who knows the matchup, and some animations start oddly enough to need getting used to.

Practical tips

TipsNotes
Gauge priorityThrow out 1,2 and 2,2,2,1+2 whenever you can to build the gauge; end combos with 1+4 whenever it fits — level 5 is what turns a match around
weak lowsUse d+1 / d/f+3(,1) / PHX 4; do not expect a pure mix-up to open anyone, bait the button out with stance pressure instead
Phoenix follow-upsMany moves go into Phoenix by holding F (4,4~F for one), so a blocked string still keeps pressure through the shift
Reversal toolkitb+3 (mid and high) plus 3+4 (low) covers every height between them; on counter hit f,f+1+2 becomes Judgment Bolt
Updraft, delayed inputThe Updraft follow-ups have a delayed-input version worth 1-2 more damage but much harder — one for when you are chasing the maximum
